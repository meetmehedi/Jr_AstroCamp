# Jr_AstroCamp: Full 5-Minute Pitch Script (300s)
**Team Mysterio | NASA International Space Apps Challenge 2026**

---

### **WHO (0:00 – 1:00): Greeting, Hook & Team**
> *"Hello everyone, welcome to the NASA International Space Apps Challenge 2026!"*
>
> *"Becoming an astronaut used to mean waiting decades for a seat on a real mission. What if you could fly one today? And what if, when you crash, you could simply try again?"*
>
> *"We are Team Mysterio, and this is **Jr_AstroCamp**: an ed-tech platform built around a game, where students don't just read about real NASA missions. They fly them, and then learn about them in the way that works best for them."*
>
> *"We're six people, including an AI automation engineer, a data scientist, an ML engineer, a full-stack developer, a researcher, and a UI/UX designer. Different skills, one shared memory: looking up at the night sky and wondering what it would take to get up there."*

---

### **WHY (1:00 – 2:00): The Problem**
> *"Here's the problem. Space content for kids usually fails in one of two ways. It's oversimplified, a cartoon that hides the real engineering trade-offs. Or it's too technical, and a young learner switches off within minutes."*
>
> *"Worse, most tools deliver everything in one format: read this, watch that. But kids don't all learn the same way. Some need to play to understand. Some need a comic. Some need to hear it out loud. Some need to write it down in their own hand. A single-format tool quietly leaves most of the classroom behind."*
>
> *"And students almost never get to fail safely. Real mission teams fail, learn, and try again. Kids get one shot and a quiz."*
>
> *"So we asked: what if the mission itself was the classroom, and every learner had a way in?"*

---

### **WHAT (2:00 – 4:00): The Game, Then The Learning Platform**

#### **The Game (2:00 – 3:10)**
> *"Meet the Campaign Map. Built with Python and Pygame at 60 frames per second, it holds 76 real, historical NASA missions, all loaded from our own missions catalog."*
>
> *"Each mission opens with a Briefing, just like a real astronaut would receive: the spacecraft, the objective, and what success requires."*
>
> *"In **Launch** missions, students manage thrust, trajectory, and timing. In **Docking**, they carefully approach and connect with a station. In **Lander** missions, they balance speed, fuel, and angle to touch down safely."*
>
> *"Then **Rover**: navigating alien terrain. **Telescope**: locking onto targets in deep space. And **Deep Space**: steering a probe toward a distant destination. Six mission types, because that variety is exactly what makes real space exploration hard."*
>
> *"After every mission, the Debrief shows your score, whether you succeeded or failed, and why. Then you replay, or fly straight into the next mission. Fail, learn, retry, exactly like real mission teams."*

#### **The Learning Platform (3:10 – 4:00)**
> *"But flying the mission is only the beginning. Around every mission sits our Learning Layer, so the same concept is reinforced however a student learns best. Short mini-games for concepts like radiation shielding and resource budgeting. Handwritten-style notebooks for students who learn by writing. Comics that tell the mission's real story. Audio lessons for listening anywhere. And short videos connecting the game back to real NASA history."*
>
> *"These formats are not separate products. They form one loop: play, get curious, read or watch, understand, and play again with new insight. Four doors into the same concept, so no student has to be the wrong kind of learner."*
>
> *"Because missions are data-driven from a catalog file, not hardcoded, and our automation pipeline keeps content fresh, we can grow the roster without rebuilding the game. Students save their progress and pick up where they left off."*

---

### **HOW (4:00 – 5:00): Impact & What We Need**
> *"Here is what works today: the simulator, all 76 missions, the full Campaign, Briefing and Debrief flow, the save system, and a companion web app in React and TypeScript."*
>
> *"Here's what we build next. An AI Mission Assistant, so a student who fails a rover mission can ask 'why did I fail?' and get a plain-language answer grounded in real physics. A Teacher Dashboard, so educators can see where students struggle and assign missions that match their curriculum. Adaptive difficulty, astronaut ranks and mission patches, multi-language support, and access beyond desktop, so any classroom on any device can join."*
>
> *"To reach that next step, we need teachers to pilot it in real classrooms, access to more NASA mission archives, and partners for the comics and audio content."*
>
> *"One day, the next person to fly a real NASA mission might be the kid who first flew it in Jr_AstroCamp. We are Team Mysterio, and we made Jr_AstroCamp: **Fly. Learn. Explore.** Thank you!"*
